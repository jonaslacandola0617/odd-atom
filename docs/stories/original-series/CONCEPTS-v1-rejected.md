# Original horror series: three concepts for discussion (2026-11-12)

> **Rejected by the user (2026-11-12):** "i dont like any of your original stories". See `CONCEPTS-v2.md`.

The user wants an original, serialized horror series with a real planned story:
"something generational", something people wait for, and the craft of films like
*The Grudge*, *Evil Dead Rise* and *Evil Dead Burn*.

- **How those films are used:** only as a bar for craft. That means dread built
  slowly, a horror with strict rules, practical-feeling physical horror,
  family stakes, and escalation that never resets.
- **What is not taken from them:** characters, creatures, curses, designs, names
  or plots.
- **Status:** nothing here is approved. The user has not chosen a direction, and
  no series bible has been written.

Every concept follows the same series rules:

- A rule system the audience can learn, test and fear.
- One ensemble with a recurring cast. Each short episode is one chapter of
  45–60 s and ends on a hook that changes the situation, not just a jump.
- A ten-episode season arc with a midpoint turn and a finale that opens Season 2.
- A path to long-form: a 10–20 min "director's cut" episode or a compiled film.

## A. "Sign-Off"

Analog, domestic dread. It fits the channel's look and the proposed name
*After Sign-Off*.

**Premise.** A regional TV station went off the air in 1994 in the middle of an
overnight broadcast, and its last night crew was never accounted for. Thirty
years later, a young archivist is hired to digitize the station's tapes. That
night, at 3:00 a.m., her family's old television turns itself on to a dead
channel. It shows a room in her own house, filmed from inside it.

**The rules (the horror's logic).**
1. The broadcast starts at 3:00 and ends at sign-off: a test card and an anthem.
2. The camera only moves while someone is watching. Turn it off and you don't
   know where it is.
3. Each night it starts one room closer to whoever it is filming.
4. If the camera reaches you before sign-off, you are "on the air". From then on
   you appear in other households' broadcasts, standing with the night crew.
5. The crew's faces are never in focus. One who is in focus is someone you know.

**Characters.**
- The archivist (26). Practical, a night owl, and the one who brought the tapes home.
- Her younger brother (14). An insomniac, he sees the broadcast first and keeps
  it secret.
- Their grandfather (80). He was the station's last night engineer. He has
  dementia, but he hums the sign-off anthem perfectly.
- The Floor Manager. The entity: a figure in a 1990s crew jacket and headset who
  cues the camera forward. We never see his face.

**Setting.** A two-storey family house, the dead station (master control,
studio, transmitter hill), and the archive's tape room.

**Season 1 arc (10 shorts).**
- Ep 1: the TV turns on and shows the kitchen. Hook: the camera pans to the
  hallway door.
- Eps 2–4: the family learns the rules the hard way. The brother watches,
  hoping to keep it still, and it moves.
- Midpoint, Ep 5: a tape shows the grandfather in 1994, alive, running from the
  studio. He left someone behind.
- Eps 6–8: the station by day. They find the crew's last log. One crew member
  in a broadcast comes into focus: the archivist's mother, who "left" when she
  was a child.
- Ep 9: the camera is outside the brother's door. The grandfather offers to watch.
- Ep 10: the grandfather goes on the air, by choice. The finale's last frame is
  a stranger's TV turning on, showing *our* family's house from inside.

**Episode hooks.** Every episode ends on where the camera is now: a room, a
door, a face starting to come into focus.

**Long-form.** The 1994 night told in full, in real time, as one feature-length
broadcast.

**Pipeline fit: best.** It uses our strengths: screens, analog damage, fixed and
moving cameras, dark houses. Rigged acting is needed only for a few figures.

## B. "Ninth Night"

A folk curse across generations. It is the most literally "generational" of the
three, and it fits the Season 2 Filipino audience if wanted.

**Premise.** In one family, at every wake, someone counts the mourners, and
there is always one more than came. On the ninth night, the extra mourner
chooses whose wake is next. Grandmother broke the rule in 1974, and every
generation since has paid for it.

**The rules.** These are invented for the series. They are not a real custom,
and the real nine-day wake and prayers are treated with respect.
1. Never leave a wake before the ninth night's prayers end.
2. The Extra Guest may only sit in an empty chair, so families fill every seat.
3. Never say the dead person's name after the ninth night. It calls them back,
   but not as themselves.
4. The Guest looks like whoever you have lost most recently.

**Characters.**
- Lola (78). She broke the rule and has been keeping a ledger of every wake since.
- Her granddaughter (22), a nursing student who doesn't believe any of it.
- The granddaughter's cousin (30), a funeral-parlour driver who has seen too much.
- A child (7) who can always count correctly.

**Setting.** A provincial ancestral house, the funeral parlour, a chapel, and
the same rooms in three eras (1974, 2003, now). The eras let us reuse each set
with different dressing.

**Season 1 arc (10 shorts).**
- Ep 1: the count is one too many. Hook: the extra chair is warm.
- Eps 2–4: one wake each in a different era, so the audience learns the rules
  through the family's history.
- Midpoint, Ep 5: we see what Lola left behind in 1974.
- Eps 6–9: the granddaughter's own family's wake, night by night. Each episode
  is one night, with the count shown on screen.
- Ep 10: the ninth night. Every chair is filled, until one mourner stands to leave.

**Episode hooks.** The count, a chair, a name almost spoken.

**Long-form.** Each season can be one family's nine nights, or a single
generation per film.

**Pipeline fit: medium.** It needs seated and standing mourners, and acting done
mostly through stillness and posture. That is reachable once we have rigged
characters.

## C. "The Extension"

Possession and body horror inside one family home. It is the closest to the
physical intensity of *Evil Dead Rise*, but has its own logic.

**Premise.** A family buys a house cheaply: the previous owner kept adding rooms
until he vanished. The house wants to be finished. At night it borrows whoever
is asleep and makes them build. Every finished room is shaped like one of them,
and once it is done, something else lives in that person.

**The rules.**
1. Anyone asleep in the house after 3 a.m. may wake up somewhere else in it,
   with cement on their hands.
2. Count the doors every morning. A new door means a new room.
3. The room's shape matches a family member. When it's finished, they come back
   wrong: same face, wrong timing, wrong joints.
4. It cannot build while two people are awake together.

**Characters.**
- A mother (40), a contractor who recognizes the building methods.
- Her husband (42), who sleeps the most.
- Their teenage daughter (16), who films everything.
- A toddler (3).
- The previous owner, now part of the walls.

**Setting.** One house that literally grows across the season. It is a set we
build once and extend each episode.

**Season 1 arc (10 shorts).**
- Ep 1: a door where there was a wall.
- Eps 2–4: the sleepwalking, and the father's hands.
- Midpoint, Ep 5: the daughter's footage shows her father building at 3:40 a.m.,
  smiling.
- Eps 6–9: the father's room is nearly finished. He comes back wrong.
- Ep 10: the mother bricks herself awake with her daughter. Then they count the
  doors.

**Episode hooks.** A new door, a measurement that doesn't add up, a family
member standing too still.

**Long-form.** The previous owner's story as a prequel film.

**Pipeline fit: hardest.** The horror is in contorted bodies and wrong movement,
so it needs the most rigging and acting work. It should come after a successful
character test.

## Recommendation (for discussion, not decided)

- **Most achievable at high quality now:** "Sign-Off" (A). It suits the
  pipeline's strengths and the channel's identity.
- **Most generational:** "Ninth Night" (B). It is the strongest family saga and
  scales well to long-form.
- **Most visceral:** "The Extension" (C), but only after the character test proves
  our rigging.
