## 1. Logline and premise

**Logline.** A broke video-transfer technician in a provincial Philippine town
secretly uploads his customers' creepiest home videos for money. Then a box of
nine unlabelled tapes arrives, prepaid in old banknotes, with no sender. Each
tape is a family's happy night that ends with the camera set down, and a still
figure standing in the dark of the room, a little closer every year.

**Premise.** Every family on the tapes lost one person within a week of the
night it was filmed, their house locked from the inside. The town blamed a
cheerful wedding videographer who was at every party. The truth is worse and
closer: the thing on the tapes travels by being **copied**. Whoever transfers
the tapes carries it, and Nico has already copied all nine. The series is the
story of a father finding out that the danger to his daughter is the work he
does with his own hands, and that the first family it took, a generation ago,
was his.

**The title.** "Transfers" means three things in the story: a video transfer
(tape to file), the thing transferring from one carrier to the next, and the
families it passes through over the years.

**The thing's name.** The old carriers' word for it is **Ang Kumuha**, "the one
who took". In Tagalog *kumuha* means both "to film" (*kumuha ng video*) and "to
take" (a person). So *"Sino'ng kumuha nito?"* asks both "Who filmed this?" and
"Who took them?" The series' question is built into its language.

## 2. Tone and the horror engine

**Tone.** Horror only. Quiet, domestic, sinister. Ordinary Filipino family life
(despedida parties, videoke, Noche Buena, a birthday with spaghetti) is filmed
warmly, and that warmth is what the horror stands in. No comedy relief, no
cute shapes, no gore. The scariest images are a still figure in a dark corner,
a camera left recording, a loved one's gentle voice saying the wrong words.

**Why it gets scarier every episode** (the writing engines from
`../CONCEPTS-v2.md`, used for their craft only):

1. **He chooses to keep watching** (*Sinister*'s engine). Nico's flaw is money,
   and the tapes pay. Each episode he plays the next tape himself, so we watch
   with him and share the guilt.
2. **It is closer every time.** Inside the tapes it stands closer each year.
   Outside them, in the recordings made around Nico (a phone, a doorbell
   camera, a camcorder on a shelf), it moves from the far doorway of his shop
   to his daughter's door. The audience can measure the danger.
3. **Rules learned one at a time.** Each episode adds one rule, and every new
   rule makes an earlier scene worse on rewatch.
4. **The wrong suspect** (*The Wailing*'s engine). The town, the detective and
   the audience blame Tito Boy, the videographer. The advice of the man we
   trust (keep hunting the man; burn the tapes) makes things worse.
5. **The system behind the curtain** (*The Cabin in the Woods*' engine). By
   the midpoint we learn this is not a haunted box; it is a chain with a
   method (copy, carry, hand over, take), and it has run for decades.
6. **The interview** (*The Silence of the Lambs*' engine). Episode 9 is a
   detective interrogating the thing itself, one question at a time, paying for
   each answer.
7. **A loved face used against you** (*Evil Dead*'s engine). The finale is one
   night in one house where the person Bea trusts most offers her the camera in
   his kindest voice.
8. **A legend that is really a warning** (*La Llorona*'s engine, inverted):
   what the families repeat ("someone was filming… who was it?") is the only
   trace the thing leaves, and it is grief, not gossip.

**Originality guardrails.** We learn from those films; we never copy them.
Specifically, TRANSFERS has **no** children who commit violence, no deity or
mask, no labelled reels of killings, no family moving into a crime house, no
"watch it and die in seven days" deadline, no cursed house that kills whoever
enters, no book of the dead and no possession by a demon. The thing takes
people silently and off screen; it is never shown hurting anyone.

## 3. The world

### The shop: NICO'S VIDEO TRANSFER
- A narrow unit in an old two-storey commercial row by the public market of an
  **unnamed provincial city in Luzon** (PROPOSAL; kept unnamed so no real town
  is implied).
- A steel roll-up shutter that sticks halfway. A hand-painted sign:
  "VIDEO TRANSFER • DUBBING • VHS / Hi8 / MiniDV → USB".
- Inside: the counter with a CRT monitor and a stack of VCRs, a capture PC, a
  stand fan, a desk lamp, shelves of customers' tapes in plastic bags with
  names on masking tape, a bench where Bea does homework, and **a dark back
  doorway** to the storeroom. The back doorway is the shop's "corner".
- GI-sheet roof: rain is loud here. The street sounds of a market town at night:
  tricycles, a distant videoke, dogs, a sari-sari store's radio.

### Home
- A small rented two-bedroom house in a barangay at the edge of the city: a
  sala with a TV and a Sto. Niño on a shelf, a narrow hallway, Bea's room
  (the window has jalousies), the couple's room. Liza installed a cheap
  doorbell camera after a break-in on the street: an unheld camera that
  watches the front path all night.

### The tapes
- **Nine VHS tapes, 1991–1999**, unlabelled, no cases, in a cardboard box with a
  prepaid waybill and an airmail envelope of old-design banknotes (₱9,000,
  "₱1,000 per tape"). A note in block letters: *PAKIKOPYA LAHAT. BAYAD NA.*
  ("Please copy them all. It's paid.")
- Every tape is a family's celebration filmed by a hired videographer, Tito
  Boy. Near the end of each, he hands the camera to someone in the family
  ("*Ikaw naman*", "Your turn"). That person films clumsily for a while and then
  **sets the camera down, still recording**, to join a photo or a song. From
  then on, a tall still figure stands in the darkest part of the room, facing
  the lens.
- Nico numbers the tapes in date order with masking tape in his own
  handwriting. That handwriting becomes a motif: in Episode 9 a tape labelled
  "10" appears, in his hand.

## 4. The entity: Ang Kumuha

### Design (from `CHATGPT-PROMPTS.md`, kept)
Tall and slightly too thin, arms too long and straight, head a little forward,
plain dark old-fashioned clothes that are hard to read. Its face is never clear:
a smear like a paused VHS frame, with darker hollows for eyes. It never moves
while anyone could see it move, with one exception: a slow tilt of the head.

### The rules (writers' canon)
1. **It appears only in a recording made with no hand on the camera.** Set a
   camera down, still recording, and it stands in the darkest part of the room
   facing the lens.
2. **In every recording, in order of date, it stands closer.**
3. **The one who was handed the camera and set it down is gone within the
   week**, the house locked from the inside. The others remember the night,
   but not who was filming.
4. **It takes only someone who accepts the camera.** The camera is always
   offered: "*Ikaw naman*." A refusal is a real defence.
5. **Whoever copies a recording of it carries it.** The carrier is the one it
   looks through: it stands near the carrier in any unheld recording. Carriers
   lose time at night and go out with a camera to offer it to someone.
6. **A carrier is released only when someone else copies the tapes, and
   released means taken.** Every shop that copied the box has closed.
7. **It comes back to the families it has filmed, a generation later.**
8. **What it wants is to be watched.** Every copy is a door, and a file has no
   last copy.
9. **It cannot stand in a room while a hand is on the camera.** Holding the
   camera keeps it out, but only for as long as you never let go.

### What the audience learns, and when

| Episode | The audience learns | The audience suspects (wrongly or early) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rule 1; the despedida father vanished, locked in | the box is haunted |
| 2 | Rule 2 (Nico's tracings) | Tito Boy, seen in a reflection, is doing it |
| 3 | Rule 3 (the son can't remember who filmed) | Tito Boy is a serial abductor (Erning's case) |
| 4 | Rule 4 ("*Ikaw naman*" on every tape) | Tito Boy is still alive and handing out cameras |
| 5 | Rule 5 (Tito Boy was a carrier and a victim) | the box can be destroyed |
| 6 | Rule 7 (Nico's own father was taken) | Liza thinks Nico is lying to her |
| 7 | Rule 6 (Erning passed it on in 1999); burning made it worse | Erning is to blame |
| 8 | a carrier offers the camera; Erning accepts it to save Bea | — |
| 9 | Rules 8 and 9 (Erning's interrogation); it began with "a first lens" | — |
| 10 | the cost of Rule 9; the upload | the long game |

### The mythology (held back; Season 2 and later)
- **The first lens.** Erning's interrogation suggests it was itself "the first
  one filmed" in this town. Tito Boy says he became a carrier in 1989 after he
  dubbed "an older tape". The origin is a single early photograph or film of
  the town (an old studio portrait hangs in Tito Boy's house in Episode 5 with
  a dark shape in its painted backdrop). PROPOSAL: Season 2 follows it back
  through the formats: studio glass negatives, a 1960s town film, Betamax.
- **The taken.** Where do the taken go? They are never found, and the families
  forget who filmed. The old banknotes that paid for the transfer are the
  despedida money from Tape 01 (the same blue airmail envelope is handed to
  Tatay Rudy in the cold open of Episode 1). Someone, or something, posted the
  taken man's money to the next shop.
- **The other boxes.** Three more shops copied the box after 1999. Their owners'
  own recordings are still somewhere.

## 5. Characters

### NICO (34): the lead
- **Want:** keep the shop alive and stop being the man his family pays for.
- **Wound:** his father "left" a week after Nico's 5th birthday in 1997, leaving
  debts. Nico grew up promising never to be like him, and now he is drowning in
  debt himself.
- **Secret:** his anonymous channel *Lumang Tape* ("Old Tape") posts the
  creepiest clips from his **customers'** private tapes without their
  permission. It is the first thing that has ever paid (₱1,840 this month).
- **Flaw that pulls him in:** he keeps watching, and keeps copying, because it
  pays.
- **Arc:** from a man who copies other people's lives for money to a man who
  holds a camera all night with both hands so his daughter does not have to. He
  ends Season 1 unable to put it down.
- **Acting:** economical movements, both hands on every tape, very still when
  scared, rubs his eyes under his glasses. His glasses reflect the screens, so
  we sometimes see a tape before he does.

### LIZA (33): his wife
- **Want:** get the family out of debt; sell the shop.
- **Wound:** she has carried the household on night-shift nurse's pay for years.
- **Secret:** she has already met a buyer for the shop's lease and has the papers
  in her locker (revealed in Episode 6).
- **Arc:** the skeptic who is right about everything except the thing itself.
  The doorbell camera in Episode 6 makes her a believer. In the finale she is the
  one who takes Bea out of the house at dawn, and Season 2 is hers as much as
  Nico's.

### BEA (9): their daughter
- **Want:** her father's attention; she sits with him in the shop after school.
- **Wound:** she hears her parents fight about money and has learned to be quiet.
- **Secret:** from Episode 4 we learn she has seen her father standing in her
  doorway at night holding a camera. He told her, "*Huwag mong sasabihin kay
  Mama*" ("Don't tell Mama"), the exact words he used about the money in
  Episode 1.
- **Arc:** from keeping secrets to refusing. She is the first to see the figure
  (Episode 1), and in the finale she says the one word that the rules allow:
  "*Ayoko po*" ("I don't want to").
- **Care:** Bea is never the threat, never harmed and never possessed.

### MANG ERNING (Ernesto, 64): the retired detective
- **Want:** to close the case that ended his career: nine families, 1991–1999,
  one person missing from each, every house locked from inside.
- **Wound:** he never found anyone.
- **Secret:** in 1999 he seized the box from Tito Boy's house and took it to a
  dubbing shop to have the tapes copied as evidence. Tito Boy vanished days
  later. Erning passed it on (revealed in Episode 7).
- **Arc:** the trusted adviser whose advice is wrong (hunt the man, then burn the
  tapes), then the confessor, then the man who accepts the camera so a child does
  not have to (Episode 8), and in his last recording the one who finds the rules
  that matter (Episode 9).
- **Voice:** dry, patient, polite to everyone, *po* to no one.

### TITO BOY (about 40 in the 1990s): the videographer, seen only on tape
- A cheerful party and wedding videographer, "Boy Lente" (Boy "Lens") to the
  town, with gelled hair, a thin moustache and a khaki vest.
- **The red herring:** he is at every party, he always hands the camera over,
  and he is seen in a TV reflection in Tape 03.
- **The truth:** he became a carrier in 1989 by dubbing an older tape. He tried
  to stop. His last tape (Episode 5) is a warning addressed to whoever copies the
  box next: "If you're watching this, you've already copied them."

### Supporting
- **JoJo (43):** Tatay Rudy's son, the boy who waves "Bye, Tay!" in Tape 01. Runs
  a vulcanizing shop. He remembers everything about the despedida except who was
  filming. His teenage daughter films everything (Rule 7, planted).
- **Nanay Cora:** Nico's mother, heard only on the phone (Episode 6).

## 6. The generational spine

What connects the tapes across families and decades:
- **One carrier per era, one hand-over per family.** The camera passed from Tito
  Boy's hands to one person at each party, 1991–1999. Each carrier was taken
  when the next person copied the box.
- **It returns a generation later.** The despedida boy's daughter films
  everything now. Nico, the five-year-old on Tape 06, is now the carrier, and his
  daughter is on Tape 10. The same families, one generation on.
- **The formats are the generations.** Glass negative, film, Betamax, VHS, Hi8,
  digital file, upload. Each transfer to a new format opened a wider door. The
  digital file and the upload are the last door: a file has no last copy, so the
  thing no longer needs one lens. Nico's flaw (the channel) is exactly the
  thing's way out.

## 7. Season 1: ten episodes

**PROPOSAL:** ten episodes of 4–6 minutes (`README.md` format). Each episode has
its own form and puts its scare in a different place, so no two play alike.
Each ends on **new information**, not just a jump.

### Ep 1 "Bayad Na" (Paid in Full), about 5:20
- **Tape:** 01, the despedida (1991).
- **Form:** real-time viewing in the shop, the tape filling the screen,
  intercut with Nico's face. A cold open inside the tape that stops just
  before anything is visible.
- **Scare placement:** at the very end, outside the tape, in a recording Nico
  made himself.
- **Turn:** Bea sees the figure first. Nico finds the father was never found, his
  house locked from inside, and he uploads the tape anyway.
- **Cliffhanger:** the phone clip he propped up to make a teaser shows the
  figure standing in his own shop's back doorway, behind his sleeping daughter.
  It tilts its head at the moment he clicks UPLOAD.
- **Clues:** "*Ikaw naman*" heard in the party noise; the blue airmail envelope;
  the old banknotes; "*Huwag mo munang sasabihin kay Mama*"; the capture starts
  just before the figure appears in the shop.

### Ep 2 "Mas Malapit" (Closer), about 4:40
- **Tapes:** 02, the videoke night; 01, 03, 05 and 07 in tracings.
- **Form:** forensic. Nico tapes clear acetate over the CRT and traces the
  doorway and the figure in marker. Tape after tape, the tracings stack up like
  a figure walking towards glass.
- **Scare placement:** the middle, inside Tape 02: the family's song ends, the
  videoke scores 100, everyone turns to the camera cheering, and the
  autofocus pulls past them onto the figure behind them, sharp, while the
  family goes soft.
- **Turn:** a home scene at dawn: Liza back from shift; Nico lies about the money;
  Bea keeps his secret.
- **Cliffhanger:** in the first seconds of Tape 03, the dark TV screen in the room
  reflects the cameraman: a smiling man in a vest, waving at his own reflection.
- **Clues:** the stacked tracings show the figure is always facing the lens,
  never the family.

### Ep 3 "Sino'ng Kumuha?" (Who Took It?), about 5:00
- **Tape:** 03, Noche Buena (cold open).
- **Form:** daylight. A doorstep investigation and one long interview at JoJo's
  vulcanizing shop. Dread by daylight, almost no music.
- **Scare placement:** dead centre, and no character sees it: one cut to JoJo's
  daughter's propped-up phone (she is filming a dance in the next room) shows,
  through the doorway in its background, the figure standing behind Nico's chair.
- **Turn:** Mang Erning arrives at the shutter with a printout of Nico's upload:
  "*Ikaw ba 'yung Lumang Tape?*" He knows the despedida house: it was his first
  case. JoJo remembers the lechon, the banner and his father's new shirt, but
  not who was filming.
- **Cliffhanger:** Erning lays down a 1999 notice, "WANTED FOR QUESTIONING",
  with the face from the TV reflection. "*Siyam na pamilya. Siyam na tape.*"
  ("Nine families. Nine tapes.")
- **Clues:** JoJo: "*'Yung anak ko ngayon, kung anu-ano kinukunan*" ("My kid
  films everything these days"), Rule 7 planted.

### Ep 4 "Ikaw Naman" (Your Turn), about 4:30
- **Tape:** 04, the binyag lunch; the audio of all nine.
- **Form:** the sound episode. Night, headphones, waveforms on the capture PC.
  Much of it is played in the dark, the picture carried by Nico's face and the
  meters.
- **Scare placement:** purely in sound, late. Under the boosted hiss of Tape 04
  after the set-down: a slow breath close to the camera's microphone (the
  entity signature). He takes the headphones off. In the silent shop, one more
  breath, from the room.
- **Turn:** "*Ikaw naman*", the same man's voice, on every tape, just before the
  hand-over (the audience heard it in Episode 1's cold open without noticing).
- **Cliffhanger:** at home, Bea, in bed: "*Pa… bakit ka po nasa kwarto ko
  kagabi? May hawak ka pong camera. Sabi mo, huwag kong sasabihin kay Mama.*"
  ("Pa… why were you in my room last night? You were holding a camera. You said
  not to tell Mama.")

### Ep 5 "Boy Lente", about 5:30 (the midpoint)
- **Tape:** Tito Boy's own last tape (1999), not from the box.
- **Form:** an exploration. Erning still has the key to Tito Boy's house, shut
  since 1999. A camcorder still stands on its tripod facing an armchair. They play
  the tape in the camcorder's own **black-and-white eyepiece viewfinder**, a tiny
  round-cornered image.
- **Scare placement:** the opening of the tape. The figure is already there,
  bending close behind Tito Boy's armchair, before he says a word. He never
  turns: "*Nandiyan na siya, 'no?*" ("It's there, isn't it?")
- **Turn:** the misdirection collapses. "*Kung napapanood mo 'to, kinopya mo na
  sila… Hindi ako ang kumuha sa kanila. Kinopya ko lang.*" ("If you're watching
  this, you've already copied them… I didn't take them. I only copied them.")
- **Cliffhanger:** a notification on Nico's phone: the capture drive has a new
  file, CAPTURE_010, made at 2:47 a.m. last night. He never made it. Its
  thumbnail is black.

### Ep 6 "Kaarawan" (Birthday), about 5:00
- **Tape:** 06, Nico's own 5th birthday (1997).
- **Form:** memory. Nico recognises the wallpaper of his childhood house, then
  his young mother, then himself. The tape is intercut with a phone call to his
  mother, who remembers the spaghetti and the clown but not who was filming.
- **Scare placement:** two-thirds in. Five-year-old Nico looks into the lens, then
  up past it at something standing behind the camera, and waves.
- **Turn:** Tito Boy hands the camera to Nico's father. His father did not leave;
  he was taken.
- **Cliffhanger:** Liza calls from the hospital with the doorbell camera's 2:47
  a.m. clip: Nico walking out of the house with a camcorder, and behind him on
  the path, the figure following. "*Nico… sino 'yung sumusunod sa'yo?*" ("Nico…
  who's following you?")

### Ep 7 "Sunog" (Fire), about 4:40
- **Tapes:** 08, Media Noche (its last minutes, as it burns); 07 in montage.
- **Form:** one night outside, behind the shop, in the rain, around a steel drum.
  Nearly one continuous scene.
- **Scare placement:** spread through the burn. As the tapes melt, the CRT inside
  (seen through the back doorway) switches itself on and plays the end of Tape
  08: the fireworks flash, and in each flash the figure is closer, until the
  picture burns out.
- **Turn:** Erning's confession: in 1999 he had the box copied for evidence. "*Ako
  ang nagpasa.*" ("I'm the one who passed it on.")
- **Cliffhanger:** Nico opens the nine files on his laptop by the fire. In every
  file's last frame the figure now fills the lens. Burning the tapes took away
  their order; in the copies it is always now.

### Ep 8 "Bantay" (Watch), about 5:00
- **Tape:** 09, the house blessing (cold open); Erning's shelf camera.
- **Form:** a stakeout in real time, 2:30 to 2:50 a.m. Erning sleeps over on the
  couch while Liza works. He sets a camcorder on a shelf facing the hallway, on
  purpose: an unheld camera will show him where it stands. The picture cuts
  between the dark house (2.39:1) and his shelf camera (4:3).
- **Scare placement:** in the stillest moment, not a jump: on the shelf camera, the
  figure stands at Bea's door. Then Nico's door opens, and Nico walks out, eyes
  open, calm and smiling.
- **Turn:** earlier that night Erning tells Bea the rule: "*Kapag may nag-abot
  sa'yo ng camera, kahit sino, kahit si Papa mo, huwag mong tatanggapin.*" ("If
  anyone hands you a camera, anyone, even your papa, don't take it.")
- **Cliffhanger:** Nico offers the camcorder: "*Mang Erning. Kayo naman po.*" Erning
  looks at Bea's door, and takes it.

### Ep 9 "Tanong" (Questions), about 5:30
- **Tape:** Erning's tape, left in the shop's shutter slot at dawn, labelled in his
  handwriting "*PARA KAY NICO. HUWAG KOKOPYAHIN.*" ("For Nico. Do not copy.")
- **Form:** the interrogation. Erning sits beside the camera he has set down in
  his apartment, facing the corner. He asks a question, switches the light off,
  and switches it on: each time the figure has changed position, and that is its
  answer. It is the slowest, quietest episode of the season.
- **Scare placement:** the last question. "*Sino'ng susunod?*" ("Who's next?")
  Light on: the corner is empty. It stands directly in front of the lens,
  filling it. Erning, gently: "*Ah. Ikaw pala.*" ("Ah. It's you.") He is
  talking to whoever watches.
- **Turn:** Rules 8 and 9: it wants to be watched; it cannot stand in a room while
  a hand is on the camera; it was "the first one filmed".
- **Cliffhanger:** Erning's apartment is locked from the inside and empty. That
  night at home, Nico finds Bea's door ajar and a camcorder on her nightstand,
  recording, red light on, facing the room, while she sleeps. He did not put it
  there. He did. On its side, masking tape, his handwriting: "10".

### Ep 10 "Hawak Ko" (I've Got It), about 6:00 (the finale)
- **Tape:** 10.
- **Form:** the tape becomes the house. Nico plays Tape 10 through the camcorder
  on the sala TV: Bea asleep; then himself coming in, setting the camera down,
  kneeling by her bed, whispering "*Anak, hawakan mo. Ikaw naman.*" Bea's eyes are
  open; she is pretending to sleep. The figure is at the lens. Then the tape's
  time stamp catches up with the clock on the wall, and **the film's letterbox
  closes to 4:3**: the real house is now a recording.
- **Scare placement:** a siege, all through the second half: the thing has his
  face and his voice. Nico loses time in cuts; each time he comes back he is
  somewhere else in the house, the camcorder in his hand, being offered.
- **Turn:** Bea in the hallway. Carrier-Nico, warm and patient: "*Anak. Ikaw
  naman.*" Bea: "*Ayoko po, Papa.*" Nico comes back to himself, and does the only
  thing the rules allow: he takes the camera in both hands and never lets go.
  "*Hawak ko.*" ("I've got it.") Black and silence.
- **Cliffhanger (the season's last minute):** dawn. Liza comes home and finds
  Nico in the sala, awake, the camcorder gripped in both hands. She takes Bea.
  On the laptop: *Lumang Tape*, "TAPE 10", uploaded at 2:47 a.m., 48,000 views
  and rising. The comments: "*bakit nasa likod ng bata*" ("why is it behind the
  kid"), "*nasa kwarto ko rin*" ("it's in my room too"). Last shot: a stranger's
  phone, propped on a table in a house we have never seen, recording, and in its
  dark corner, a figure standing very still.

### The finale and the long game
- **Season 1 answer:** what the thing does, how it travels, who it took from
  Nico, and how to keep it out (hold the camera).
- **Season 1 cost:** Nico can never put the camera down; Erning is gone; and the
  thing has left the one-lens world for an upload with no last copy.
- **Long-game questions:** Where are the taken? Who posted the despedida money?
  What was "the first lens"? What is on the other shops' tapes? What happens when
  thousands of viewers set their phones down?
- **PROPOSAL, Season 2:** told from Liza's side. A nurse who keeps a hand on
  everything, chasing the first lens back through the town's oldest photographs
  while the viewers start to vanish.

## 8. Recurring motifs, and visual and sound grammar

### Motifs
- **The hand-over.** Hands passing a camera, always filmed close. "*Ikaw naman*."
- **The corner and the doorway.** Every location has one dark doorway; the
  audience learns to watch it.
- **The red REC light.** A tiny red dot is the series' scariest colour.
- **The paused-frame face.** Its face is smeared like a paused tape, even in
  phone and file recordings.
- **Glasses.** Screens reflect in Nico's lenses; it is sometimes where we see a
  recording first.
- **Masking-tape numbers in Nico's handwriting.** 01–09, then a 10 he doesn't
  remember writing.
- **Money.** Old banknotes, the blue airmail envelope, the channel's earnings
  counter. The thing pays.
- **The half-open shutter.** The shop is never fully closed.
- **2:47 a.m.** The carrier's lost minutes. (PROPOSAL: Nico's cheap digital
  watch beeps the hour, so its silence at 2:47 is a cue.)

### Visual grammar (PROPOSAL, inside the format in `README.md`)
- **Real scenes: 2.39:1 letterbox,** composed, mostly locked-off or slow. Lit by
  practical sources: the desk lamp, the CRT, a fluorescent tube, the street.
- **Tapes: 4:3,** full height, with VHS damage, a date stamp, colour bleed and
  tracking noise. Tape picture is warmer and softer than the real world.
- **Phone and doorbell recordings:** full 16:9 frame. The black bars come off,
  so the audience feels exposed: an unletterboxed frame always means "a
  recording with no hand on it".
- **The figure is never in the 2.39:1 frame.** In Season 1 it exists only in
  recordings. When a character turns to the real doorway, it is empty. The
  rule is broken once: in the finale the letterbox closes to 4:3 as the house
  becomes a recording.
- **Stillness.** It never moves on screen except the head tilt. Scares come from
  framing, focus and time (it is somewhere new after a cut, a dropout or a
  light switch), never from it running.
- **Faces:** a small set of painted expressions per character (README); in the
  scariest moments the faces hold still and the eyes carry it.

### Sound grammar (per `SOUND.md`)
- **Three layers in every scene:** ambience of the place (rain on GI sheet, the
  fan, the market street at night, the hospital PA through a phone), foley for
  every visible action (cassettes, the VCR flap, the shutter, glasses on a
  counter) and the horror layer.
- **The entity signature: the focus hunt.** When it is in frame, the camera's
  autofocus motor whirs, searching, and the picture breathes soft-sharp-soft:
  the camera is focusing on something. Under it, a slow warped tape breath
  (library `tape-breath`). The signature crosses from the tapes to the phone in
  Episode 1, and in the finale it is heard in the real house.
- **Silence:** before every reveal the world drops out one sound at a time: the
  fan, then the rain, then the CRT's whine. The audience feels the room go
  wrong before it sees why.
- **Tape audio sounds like tape:** mono, band-limited, wow and flutter, the
  camcorder's handling noise and motor whine, party voices compressed and hissy.
- **No score inside the tapes.** In real scenes the horror layer is a low drone
  and sub swells, and never a melody. Party music on the tapes is original and
  composed for us (no real songs).
- **Dialogue first,** Taglish, designed voices, at −14 LUFS.

## 9. Care

- **Fiction only.** No real people, families, crimes, victims or disappearances.
  The town is unnamed; all names are invented, and real surnames are not used
  for the missing. The Facebook-style missing-persons group in Episode 1 is
  invented, and is shown only briefly with invented names.
- **No "true story" claims** anywhere in the series or its posts. It is presented
  as fiction.
- **No dares and no chain-letter framing.** Nothing in the episodes or their
  posts tells real viewers to watch at a certain hour, share to stay safe, set
  their phone down or look for the figure in their own rooms. The in-story
  channel's comments are part of the fiction.
- **Nothing that instructs harm.** No methods, no violence shown, no gore. The
  taking always happens off screen and is told only by an empty, locked house.
- **Children:** Bea and the children on the tapes are never harmed, threatened
  physically or possessed, and never act against anyone.
- **Privacy, inside the story:** Nico's uploading of customers' private tapes is
  shown as a wrong, and it has consequences.
- **Our own work only.** No characters, creatures, artefacts, plots or set pieces
  from the reference films. No real songs, broadcast footage or brands on screen.

## 10. Production notes (for when work resumes)

- **Cast on screen at once:** at most three speaking characters (usually two).
  Tape party crowds are four to six simple background figures, mostly seen from
  behind, reused with palette changes.
- **Locations, Season 1:** the shop (all ten episodes), home (2, 4, 6, 8, 10), JoJo's
  shop (3), Tito Boy's house (5), the shop's back lot (7), Erning's apartment (9),
  plus one tape living room per tape (simple, reusable walls and props).
- **Voices (PROPOSAL):** designed voices for Nico, Liza, Bea, Erning and Tito Boy,
  plus a few one-scene tape voices. Credits are to be estimated per episode and
  checked against the balance before anything is generated (`README.md`).
- **Sound:** library v1 (`public/assets/audio/transfers/sfx-v1/`) covers many cues;
  each episode's screenplay lists the new sounds it needs.
- Character modelling is paused; nothing here changes the models.
