# Original horror series: concepts v2 (2026-11-12)

## Context

**The user's direction.** The v1 concepts were rejected. The user wants the
writing quality of *The Wailing*, *The Curse of La Llorona*, *The Silence of the
Lambs*, *The Cabin in the Woods*, *Evil Dead*, *Evil Dead Rise* and *Evil Dead
Burn*, and above all *Sinister* 1 and 2. The tone is horror only, in our dark
analog look, with better characters.

**How we use those films.** We study why their writing works, not what happens
in them. None of their characters, creatures, curses, artefacts, names,
set-pieces or twists is used.

## What those films do (the craft we are aiming for)

| Film | The engine we learn from |
|---|---|
| *Sinister* 1–2 | **The protagonist chooses to keep watching.** His flaw (ambition, money) pulls him in. The evidence is media he plays himself, so the audience watches with him. Each reel is its own set piece. The danger is already inside his home, planted early. |
| *The Wailing* | **Misdirection.** The town blames the obvious suspect. The person we trust gives the advice that dooms. A desperate parent has to choose whom to believe, under a clock. |
| *La Llorona* | **A folk legend with grief at its centre.** Water, a mother's cry, and a warning that families have repeated for generations. |
| *The Silence of the Lambs* | **The interview.** A young professional and a brilliant, confined monster, trading information in exchange for something personal. A missing person sets the clock. |
| *The Cabin in the Woods* | **The rules behind the curtain.** The audience finds out the horror is a system, and the system has a purpose. |
| *Evil Dead*, *Rise*, *Burn* | **An object that wakes something, then possession and siege.** The worst thing is a loved one's face and voice used against you, in one confined space through one night. |

**Writing rules for our series.** Each line below takes its idea from one of the
films above.
- A lead with a want and a flaw that makes them walk further in (*Sinister*).
- Rules the audience learns one at a time.
- One misdirection planted from Episode 1 (*The Wailing*).
- A twist that is fair on rewatch.
- Set pieces that escalate.
- Every short episode ends on new information, not just a jump scare.

## 1. "TRANSFERS"

*Sinister*-grade dread, made for our analog look.

**Logline.** A broke video-transfer technician starts secretly uploading the
creepiest home videos his customers bring in. Then a box of unlabeled tapes
arrives with a prepaid fee and no return address.

**The lead.**
- Nico (34) runs a failing shop that turns old VHS and Hi8 home videos into
  files.
- His flaw: he's drowning in debt, and his anonymous channel of "found tapes"
  is the first thing that has ever paid.
- His wife wants to sell the shop. Their daughter (9) sits with him after school.

**The box.** Nine tapes, each an ordinary family evening: a birthday, a
karaoke night, a wake, a baptism. Every tape ends the same way. Someone sets
the camera down, still recording, and in the dark corner of the room a figure
stands very still, facing the lens. Nico looks up each family; each one
vanished within days of its tape. No bodies, no struggle, doors locked from
inside.

**The rules (learned one per episode).**
1. The figure only appears after the camera has been set down: no one is
   holding it.
2. It is closer to the lens in every tape, in order of their dates.
3. The family member who set the camera down is never found. The others are
   found years later, alive, unable to remember who filmed.
4. Every tape was transferred before, by a previous technician. The box has
   been through other shops, and every one of them closed.

**The misdirection (our *Wailing* move).** All nine tapes were filmed by the same
man: a wedding videographer, seen as a reflection in a TV screen in tape 3. The
town remembers him and blames him. A retired detective helps Nico hunt him.

**The twist (planted in Episode 1, fair on rewatch).** The videographer was the
first victim. The thing does not live in the tapes; it lives in whoever
transfers them. At night, Nico has been leaving his shop with a camera. The
tenth tape in the box was not there when it arrived, and it is of his own
living room, filmed from where his daughter sleeps.

**Season 1 (10 shorts).** Each episode is one tape, a self-contained set piece
in the found footage, and each ends on the frame where the figure is found.
Viewers rewatch to spot it early.
- Midpoint, Ep 5: the detective finds the videographer's own last tape.
- Ep 9: Nico's daughter hands him a camera she found in the shop, still warm.
- Ep 10: Nico watches tape 10 and sees himself set the camera down.

**Long-form.** The previous technician's story; or Season 2, told from the
detective's side.

**Why it fits us.** Tapes, screens, transfer decks and VHS damage are our home
ground. A single still figure is horror our rigs can sell perfectly. The
characters need to act in real rooms: sitting, threading tapes, reacting,
creeping at night.

## 2. "THE CRYING SEASON"

The engines of *La Llorona* and *The Wailing*: a folk legend, misdirection and a
father under a clock.

**Logline.** In a river town, every rainy season a woman is heard crying by the
water, and mothers bolt their doors. When a police officer's son starts saying
"the crying lady says sorry", the town's revered healer offers to save him.

**The lead.**
- Ramon, a small-town police officer: tired, superstitious only when no one is
  looking, and a loving, impatient father.
- His mother-in-law believes everything. A new nurse at the town clinic is the
  outsider the town distrusts.

**The legend (invented for this series).** A drowned mother cries along the river
before children go missing. Families hang wet clothes on their doors so she
passes by.

**The misdirection.**
- The whole town, and the audience, fears the crying woman, and the town blames
  the outsider nurse.
- The truth, planted from Episode 1: she cries *before* a child is taken, never
  after. She is warning them.
- What takes the children is a pact the town's elders renew every flood season.
- The healer is its keeper. His "protection" rituals mark the next child.

**Set pieces.**
- Crying under the house at 2 a.m.
- Wet footprints that go *into* the river, not out of it.
- A healing ritual in the rain.
- A flooded church.
- A boat search through drowned houses.
- The son sleepwalking to the water while the crying grows desperate behind him.

**Season 1 arc.**
- Eps 1–3: the crying, the missing child, the blame.
- Ep 4: the healer's first ritual.
- Midpoint, Ep 5: Ramon hears the crying *inside* his house, standing over his
  son's bed, and it sounds like grief, not hunger.
- Eps 6–8: the nurse finds the clinic's flood-season records, which show the
  same pattern every 12 years.
- Ep 9: Ramon must choose between the healer's last rite and the crying woman.
- Ep 10: he follows the crying.

**Episode hooks.** Each ends on a sign that turns the meaning of the crying.

**Long-form.** The first flood season the pact began, told in full.

## 3. "THE VISITING HOURS"

The interview from *The Silence of the Lambs*, with *Evil Dead*'s possession and
siege.

**Logline.** A psychology intern is assigned to interview a man in a provincial
jail who confessed to killing a family he cannot have reached. He says he was
"worn", and he will tell her where the missing daughter is, one answer per
visit, if she reads him the letters he is no longer allowed to receive.

**The leads.**
- Ana (26). Brilliant, underpaid, hiding a mother in a care home.
- The prisoner (50s). Gentle, precise, frightened of himself, and he knows
  things about Ana's mother.

**The rules (learned in the interviews).**
1. "It" moves into whoever listens longest.
2. It cannot cross water it hasn't been invited over.
3. When it leaves a person, they keep its memories for a while. That is how the
   prisoner knows where the girl is.

**The engine.** Quid pro quo. Each visit, he gives one true clue to the missing
daughter. Ana gives something in return: a letter, a name, a memory of her own.
He gets calmer and more ordinary, and she gets something else.

**Misdirection.** Everyone, the audience included, watches the prisoner for the
thing. The guard who has worked his block for 20 years is never questioned.

**The siege (our *Evil Dead* finale).** A typhoon blackout locks down the jail
overnight with Ana inside. One by one, the people she trusts start speaking
in her mother's voice.

**Season 1 arc.**
- Eps 1–4: visits and clues.
- Ep 5: the missing girl is found alive, and she asks for Ana by name.
- Eps 6–9: the typhoon night.
- Ep 10: dawn, and Ana walks out. We are no longer sure who is walking.

**Episode hooks.** Each visit ends with the prisoner's answer, and the cost
revealed in the last frame.

**Long-form.** The interviews play well as long, slow-burn episodes.

## Recommendation (for discussion, not decided)

**"TRANSFERS"** is closest to what the user loves most (*Sinister*). It is also
built on our analog strengths, and its one-tape-per-episode structure makes
viewers come back for the next tape.
