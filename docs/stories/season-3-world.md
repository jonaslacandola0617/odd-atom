# Season 3: famous ghost cases from around the world (Episodes 21–30)

**The user's brief (2026-11-12, paraphrased):** "Famous super really scary ghost stories around the world." "I want a really really scary ghost video, make sure that it is scary." Then, relayed: "And don't forget I want real story." and "I want something true to life super scary ghost video."

**The user's criticism of Season 2:** every episode had the same shape (intro → story → a hint of the ghost in the middle → silence → one lit jump-scare reveal → aftermath → loop). "It's too predictable … each video should be unique, not a reused template."

## Season rules

- **Real cases only.** Every episode is a real place with a real, documented account: witness testimony, investigator reports, press coverage, photographs, CCTV or recordings. No folklore-only tales (La Llorona and Kuchisake-onna are out).
- **True to life.** Ordinary rooms, real times of night, real sounds (knocking, footsteps, a door, a voice on a tape), what witnesses actually described. Restraint: the horror is that it seems real. No monster designs.
- **Honest facts.** Each episode says what was reported and by whom, and what is disputed or debunked. Facts below are from **web-search summaries only** (full pages, books and primary records were not read); re-check them in a full source before posting.
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs **Jack – Deep Dark Horror Narrator** (`BVPhWQykET1TSvfGzrUr`, `eleven_v4`) for every episode (all are reported cases), on the user's Starter account (flows created fresh in this session). English narration and captions.
- **No shared template.** Each episode below has its own form, scare placement, sound language and visual language. No two share the Season 2 shape. Shared helpers (PS1 materials, `entities.tsx`, `horror_score.py`) are fine; shot plans, pacing and scare design are new each time.
- **Standards that stay:** the hook in under a second; 45–60 s; hidden rewatch details; the dark key (≤45/255 outside deliberate light); the presence and line-margin checks (for an episode with no score, the "music" measured is the whole non-voice bed); −14 LUFS; decoded peak ≤ −1 dBFS; the checked `mix.m4a` muxed over the picture.
- **Care:** no gore; no bodies of real victims; no naming or accusing living private people (children in these cases are never named); deaths, murders and suicides handled with care (no method, no glamour); no trespass or "go there at night" dares; no copyrighted film or game designs (nothing from *The Conjuring* films or any other adaptation).
- **Look:** the channel's pixel-3D analog style (180×320, PS1 shaders), pushed towards realism: recording feel, realistic scale and lighting, believable framing.

## The motion rule (the user, after Episodes 21 and 22)

"The sequence is different for each now but there's a lack of motion … a static video like this, only the narrator is telling the story. The video itself should be scary, the video itself should tell the story … not like the first two episodes."

From Episode 23 on, every episode must:
- **move:** every shot has camera motion (tracking, creeping, orbiting, handheld pushes, point-of-view walks); no locked-off or time-lapse episode;
- **show the story:** each scene has at least one visible event (something moves, opens, falls, turns, is pulled, appears) that tells the beat with the sound off;
- **pass the mute test:** with narration removed, a viewer can follow what happens and is frightened by the picture;
- **use real-sounding effects:** ElevenLabs sound effects (`eleven_text_to_sound_v2`) are available on the Starter account at about 17 credits a variation, for the sounds the picture depends on.

Episodes 21 and 22 are kept as delivered; the user has not asked for them to be redone.

## The episodes

### 21. "Watch the Chair": the Enfield poltergeist (London, 1977)

- **The story:** from August 1977 a council house in Enfield, north London, home to a mother and four children, was the centre of reported knocking in the walls, furniture moving and, later, a gruff old man's voice that appeared to come from one of the daughters. Investigators recorded there for more than a year.
- **Claim versus documented (search summaries):**
  - Documented: the house at 284 Green Street; a police visit around 1 a.m. in which WPC Carolyn Heeps reported (and later stated on record) that she saw a chair move about three to four feet across the living-room floor by itself and found no wires (SPR encyclopedia and others; officer's name spelled Heeps or Heaps). Recordings, mainly by Maurice Grosse and Guy Lyon Playfair, total over 180 hours. Daily Mirror photographer Graham Morris took the well-known bedroom photographs.
  - Disputed: SPR investigators Anita Gregory and John Beloff concluded the children faked activity; skeptics attribute the voice to false vocal cords and note a child's vocabulary; one daughter later said on ITV that they faked it "once or twice". Grosse believed there was no trickery. No independent verdict exists.
  - **Not used:** the "levitation" photo claims; the voice's claimed identity; any quotation of the real tapes.
- **Care:** the children are never named and never shown (shapes under blankets). The family is not mocked; the skeptic view is given. No real recording is used; the voice is an indistinct synthesized rasp.
- **Structure:** one unbroken, locked-off 55-second take of a dark back bedroom, as if a camera had been left running. The hook tells you to **watch the chair**. The chair never moves while you can see it: it moves only during tape dropouts, a little closer to the bed each time, turning to face it.
- **Scare mechanism:** misdirection and "moves when you look away" inside a single frame; many small scares (knocks that answer, a shadow crossing the landing light, a rasp from the far bed); the last frame shows someone sitting in the chair, facing the bed, before the dropout loops to frame 0.
- **Sound:** diegetic only: room tone, the tape recorder's hiss and motor, knocks inside the wall, a clock, a dog far off, a car passing, a chair leg scraping only under the dropouts.

### 22. "Eighty-Eight Years": the Okiku doll (Mannenji, Iwamizawa, Hokkaido)

- **The story:** a traditional doll left at Mannenji temple; its hair, short at first, is reported to have grown to about 25 cm and to keep growing after trims.
- **Claim versus documented:** reported by the temple (as relayed in press and blogs): the Suzuki family left the doll there in 1938 when they moved. Unverified: a 1918 purchase by a young man named Eikichi; the claim that a strand was tested as a child's hair; a 1955 date. Skeptics suggest the fibres relaxing or human intervention.
- **Care:** the girl the doll is named after is never shown; her death (reported as illness) is stated gently or not at all. The doll is an original design, not any film doll.
- **Structure:** one fixed frame of the shrine, years counting from 1938 to now; seasons and light change; the hair lengthens; the doll's head turns towards the lens by a degree a decade.
- **Scare mechanism:** none in the usual sense. The horror is the realisation at the end: the year counter reaches the present and the doll is facing you.
- **Sound:** one temple bell, struck once a decade, and silence.

### 23. "Backwards": the Bell Witch (Adams, Tennessee, 1817–1821)

- **The story:** a Tennessee farm family reported scratching, knocking and a voice that spoke, sang and tormented John Bell, who died in December 1820.
- **Claim versus documented:** most details come from M. V. Ingram's 1894 book, which quotes Richard Williams Bell's account ("Our Family Trouble"); that manuscript has never been found. Accounts agree on a vial found by John Bell and the cat that died after tasting it, but an 1880 sketch differs and Skeptoid argues Ingram may have invented the death story. The death date is given as 19 or 20 December 1820.
- **Care:** John Bell's death is never shown; no poisoning method is described beyond "a vial no one recognised".
- **Structure:** told backwards, scene by scene, each joined by a film rewind: the funeral, the sickroom and the vial, supper when the voice talked, the children's room, outside in 1817, the first night. Every shot moves; every scene has a visible event.
- **Scare mechanism:** at the front: a laugh and a gust that snuffs the lanterns at about 5.7 s; then several small ones (a vial rolling, an empty chair's shadow, a quilt yanked, scratches appearing).
- **Sound:** ElevenLabs sound effects (laugh, gust, quilt, scratching), synthesized foley, a drone with reversed swells into each rewind.

### 24. "They Turn Their Heads": the Island of the Dolls (Xochimilco, Mexico City)

- **The story:** on a chinampa in the canals of Xochimilco, Don Julián Santana Barrera hung hundreds of dolls over decades; he said they were for a girl who drowned nearby. He died in 2001, reportedly in the same canal.
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the island, the dolls, Don Julián's long life there and his death in 2001. Legend: the drowned girl (sources disagree on her age, her relation to him and what happened; some say he imagined it); the dolls whispering and moving, as visitors report.
- **Care:** the girl and Don Julián's death are told gently, never shown. It is a working tourist site: no dare, no trespass. Respect for a real family that runs it.
- **Structure:** first person, "you are in the boat", one gliding take along the canal and back.
- **Scare mechanism:** several small ones: on the way back, dolls that faced the water now face you.
- **Sound:** water, the pole, wind, and the creak of hundreds of dolls on wire.

### 25. "Camera Three": Hampton Court Palace (October 2003)

- **The story:** on several days in October 2003, guards were called to a fire door near Clock Court that kept opening. On the CCTV, the doors flew open with no visible cause; on another day a robed figure appeared and closed them.
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the palace released the footage; Historic Royal Palaces has said there is no explanation. Disputed: a costumed visitor or a staff prank (the palace did not call it a hoax). Not used: the "Skeletor" nickname, and blog details (kitchens, bony hands) that conflict with the palace's account.
- **Care:** no living staff named; no historical figure claimed.
- **Structure:** a guard's night patrol, handheld torch POV, cut against the CCTV tiles in the control room (revised for the motion rule).
- **Scare mechanism:** the middle: the doors burst open in front of him; on the CCTV tile behind him, a figure stands where he just walked, almost missed.
- **Sound:** near silence: the hum of a monitor room; the doors are the only loud sound.

### 26. "Post Three": the Castle of Good Hope (Cape Town)

- **The story:** the oldest colonial building in South Africa, with reports from soldiers, staff and visitors of footsteps, a woman in grey running through the corridors, and the story of Governor Pieter Gijsbert van Noodt, found dead in 1729 (some say 1728) on the day he sentenced seven soldiers to hang.
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the castle and van Noodt's death (cause disputed: a curse in legend, a heart attack in history). Reported: guard and staff accounts of footsteps and the Lady in Grey; a female skeleton found in excavation, linked to her only by suspicion. Most ghost sources are tourism pages.
- **Care:** the hanged soldiers are not shown; no curse is presented as fact.
- **Structure:** a night guard's radio log, first person (Jack as the guard), torch POV.
- **Scare mechanism:** several small, escalating: footsteps that stop when he stops; a grey figure at the far end of a corridor; control's last message on the radio display.
- **Sound:** radio squelch, boots on stone, wind off the bay.

### 27. "The Ball": Waverly Hills Sanatorium (Louisville, Kentucky)

- **The story:** a tuberculosis hospital (from 1910, the big building from 1926), later abandoned, where ghost investigators report voices, shadow figures and a child who plays with a ball.
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the sanatorium and its tunnel (about 500 ft, used to move supplies and the dead out of sight). Disputed: the death toll (claims of 63,000+ are not supported; a former medical director's memoir is cited for a far lower figure). Room 502's suicides are local legend, not documented, and are **not used**.
- **Care:** no patients shown; TB deaths stated with respect; no suicide material.
- **Structure:** a handheld night-vision walk down the ward corridor (revised for the motion rule); the investigators' ball rolls away from the camera, the camera follows it room by room.
- **Scare mechanism:** the scare never comes. The ball stops, then rolls back to the camera. Nothing else happens. The horror is the size of the hand that would have rolled it.
- **Sound:** silence-heavy: the camera's fan, a building settling, the ball on concrete.

### 28. "Developing": the Brown Lady of Raynham Hall (Norfolk, 1936)

- **The story:** on 19 September 1936, Country Life photographers Hubert Provand and Indre Shira photographed the staircase at Raynham Hall; the print shows a veiled figure descending. Published in Country Life (26 December 1936) and Life (4 January 1937).
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the photograph and its publication; experts for Country Life said the negative was not tampered with. Disputed: double exposure (Joe Nickell; a pale line above each tread), grease on the lens, or a superimposed statue.
- **Care:** no named living person; the house is a private home, no visiting framing.
- **Structure:** a reconstruction of the photographers on the staircase in 1936 (revised for the motion rule): a moving camera climbs the stairs; each flash shows the figure a few steps lower; then the darkroom, where the print develops.
- **Scare mechanism:** the last frame: the print keeps developing after it should have stopped, and the figure is at the bottom of the stairs, at the lens.
- **Sound:** shutters, the flash's whine, footsteps on old stairs, then the darkroom drip.

### 29. "Checkout Is at Ten": Monte Cristo Homestead (Junee, NSW)

- **The story:** a Victorian homestead built in the 1880s by Christopher Crawley, called Australia's most haunted house, where guests reported footsteps, voices, being touched and a woman in black on the balcony (said to be Mrs Crawley).
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the house, the Crawley family and the Ryan family's restoration; years of guest and tour reports and a TV investigation's recordings. Not verified: the specific tragic stories (a maid's fall, a burned stable boy and others), which are **not used**. The house has been closed to the public since January 2025 and is private property: no visiting framing.
- **Structure:** second person in real time: you are the overnight guest, 2:58 a.m. to 3:01 a.m.
- **Scare mechanism:** bookends: a wall of sound at the start that cuts to nothing, and the last frame.
- **Sound:** a wall of noise (rain, wind, the house) that drops to dead silence.

### 30. "What They Tell You": Poveglia (Venetian Lagoon), the finale

- **The story:** an island in the lagoon, used as a quarantine station (1793–1814) and later a hospital until 1968, now closed and abandoned, and famous online as the world's most haunted island.
- **Claim versus documented:** documented: the quarantine station and the hospital/long-term care facility, closed in 1968; the island is closed to visitors. Legend and **not documented**: the asylum doctor who threw himself from the bell tower, lobotomies, and "160,000 plague dead" soil. Historians dispute much of the popular story.
- **Care:** no patients shown; the suicide legend is named as a legend and not depicted.
- **Structure:** an unreliable narrator: the narrator tells the famous legend as tourists repeat it; the picture and captions correct each claim with the record; then something small happens that the record cannot correct.
- **Scare mechanism:** the last frame.
- **Sound:** bells and water building to a wall of noise, then the real island's silence.
