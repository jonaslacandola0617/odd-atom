## Facts to have ready if commenters ask

- On the night of 21 August 1986, Lake Nyos, a crater lake in north-west Cameroon, suddenly released a large cloud of carbon dioxide (a limnic eruption).
- CO₂ is about 1.5 times as dense as air. The cloud flowed down the valleys and displaced the air near the ground; 1,746 people and about 3,500 livestock died.
- The gas came from volcanic sources below and had built up, dissolved, in the deep water.
- What triggered the release (a landslide, cooling or wind, or a small volcanic input) is still debated.
- A degassing pipe has drawn gas from the bottom since 2001, with two more added around 2011.
- Lake Monoun, nearby, had a smaller eruption in 1984. Lake Kivu, on the Rwanda–DR Congo border, holds large amounts of dissolved gas and is monitored.
- Search summaries only; re-check the numbers and dates before quoting them.
