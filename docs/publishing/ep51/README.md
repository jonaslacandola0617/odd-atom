## What the cut is built for

- **First second:** bright summer, a hedge taller than a man, and a white hat gliding along its top.
- **Scare 1:** the hat stops in front of you; the cicadas go silent; two dark eyes rise over the hedge.
- **Scare 2:** the sealed room at night: the salt in the bowl turns black (watch it before he says it); nails tap on every papered window at once.
- **Scare 3:** Grandpa's voice at the door, gentle, asking to be let in; on the paper, the shadow of someone far too tall folds down at the waist until her head is at a man's height.
- **The turn:** Grandpa is sitting beside me, shaking his head.
- **Scare 4 (the stinger):** the newspaper rips off the window: her face, sideways, black eyes.
- **The end:** dawn, she walks beside the car bent double, her hand at my window. I didn't look.
- **The loop:** the hat on the hedge again.
- **Hidden details:**
  - the shadow's head bobs with each word the voice says;
  - for two frames in the silent night, her folded shadow is already on the screen;
  - her steps on the gravel keep exact time with the car.

## Captions (the burned-in English)

1. The summer I turned fifteen, I watched a white hat drift along the top of my grandparents' hedge. That hedge is eight feet tall.
2. When I told Grandpa, the colour left his face. By dark they had sealed me upstairs. Paper over every window. A bowl of salt in each corner.
3. "Don't open that door before sunrise. Whatever you hear."
4. Around two, the salt started turning black. Then something tapped on every window at once. On the second floor.
5. Then, through the door, Grandpa's voice.
6. (through the door) "It's all over now. You can come out. Open the door for Grandpa."
7. But Grandpa was sitting right beside me. And he was shaking his head.
8. At dawn they drove me out of the valley. She walked beside the car the whole way, bent down to my window. I didn't look. Not once.
