# Reference study: title design and story-first text

This revision responds to the user's direction to remove text that does not help tell the story and to study the supplied title outlines more closely.

## Evidence inspected

All eight uploaded clips were compared at 1.3 seconds for their opening composition and lettering. Flight, Skeleton, Brain at Birth, and New Moon were then compared at 0, 0.18, 0.6, 1.2, 2.5, and 3.2 seconds to inspect opening-title timing. This is targeted visual analysis of supplied files, not a claim of exhaustive frame-by-frame mastery or a survey of the whole channel.

| Reference | Observed title treatment | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Could Humans Evolve Flight | Rounded heavy lettering; pale faces; nested yellow/gold and orange borders; warm offset depth; slightly tilted block; mixed case with emphatic “FLY?” | Build the title using actual separate stroke passes, with a rounded face and deliberate hierarchy. |
| Your Skeleton Is Electric | Purple letter faces with a pale outer border, sitting directly over the lifting action | Choose colors for the scene and preserve separation from background action. |
| Your Brain Peaked at Birth | Cyan/green faces, blue/purple intermediate border, deep-purple outer edge; smaller connective text | Layered lettering is a designed graphic object, not a plain heading. |
| Let’s Steal a New Moon | Dark faces, light broad border, darker outside separation; smaller setup above the main question | A space title can be compact and legible without persistent interface decorations. |
| Your Brain on Magic Mushrooms | Multiple nested bright contours surround the stacked lettering, forming a connected title silhouette | Paint outer contours first and letter faces last so overlaps remain clean. |
| The Deadliest Thing in Your Kitchen | Black lettering on angled yellow ribbons | The samples use several title systems; there is no single universal outline recipe. |
| The True Cost of War | Colored depth/shadow lettering inside an illustrated projection screen | Integrate text with a scene element when the story supports it. |
| Where Did the Antimatter Go? | Compact tilted lettering with warm layered borders and depth against a dark cosmic figure | Preserve contrast and shape at phone scale. |

In the four opening timing comparisons, the question or claim is already readable in the first frame and remains over character action through approximately 2.5 seconds. By 3.2 seconds, Flight, Skeleton, and Brain at Birth have moved into their explanation; New Moon's title is fading as the selected moon becomes the subject. Exact transitions vary by clip.

The source clips contain some relevant diagram labels and occasional qualifications. That does not justify adding unrelated branding, episode numbers, progress indicators, taglines, or repeated headline summaries. The user's current direction is to keep those out of our videos.

## Changes to the pilot

- Remove the brand, episode badge, footer, tagline, timer, progress line, “Ground Control” label, schematic note, redundant Earth label, legend pills, explanatory headline sequence, and caption panel.
- Use an opening title with four separately painted contour/shadow passes and a light face, rounded mixed-case lettering, compact stacking, and a slight tilt. Nunito is an original font choice, not an identified source font.
- Make the full opening question visible at frame zero; retain only the brief “They do.” answer treatment before moving to the illustration.
- Move the orbital explanation upward and enlarge Earth slightly to make room for the story rather than reserving space for headings.
- Use plain short white narration captions with a dark edge, directly over the artwork.
- Keep the arrow directions causal: gravity points toward Earth's center; motion is tangent to the orbit.
