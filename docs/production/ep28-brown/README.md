# Episode 28: "Developing" (v1)

**Delivery:** `assets/videos/ep28-brown-v1/ep28-developing.mp4` (1080×1920, 30 fps, 57.2 s, H.264 two-pass tuned for grain, AAC 192k) with its poster `ep28-developing-poster.png` (frame 60: the print surfacing in the red tray). Size and hash are in "Review of the encoded file". It has **no user approval** and **no listening pass**.

**The case:** the Brown Lady of Raynham Hall, Norfolk (#28 in `docs/stories/season-3-world.md`). A real, documented photograph: on 19 September 1936 two Country Life photographers photographed the main staircase, and the print shows a veiled figure coming down it.

## Why this episode looks and sounds like this

- **Rules:** it is built to the motion rule and the no-gimmick-transitions rule (hard cuts only).
- **Form:** two times cut hard against each other:
  - the staircase hall in 1936, in black and white with gate weave and dust, lit by a lamp and then by magnesium flash;
  - the darkroom under the red safelight, where the print develops in the tray.
- **New in the season:** a scene seen on the **view camera's ground glass**, upside down and reversed, as such a camera sees, framed by the focusing cloth and its grid.
- **The picture tells it (the mute test):**
  1. **The darkroom (the hook):** the print rocks in the tray, and the image surfaces: a staircase and a pale shape on it.
  2. **The house at night:** a motor car's headlights sweep across the gabled front.
  3. **The hall:** the view camera on its tripod and the photographer under the cloth; the assistant raises the flash pan; **the first flash**. Nothing is on the stairs.
  4. **The ground glass, upside down:** a pale vapour at the top of the stairs **comes down and becomes a woman in a veil**.
  5. **The second flash: she is lit on the stairs.** Then the lamp comes on: **the stairs are empty**, and the photographer lifts his head from the cloth.
  6. **The darkroom:** **she comes up out of the paper**. The tongs carry the print to the stop bath.
  7. **The staircase by moonlight**, climbing: **a figure stands on the landing against the window**; then she is not there.
  8. **The stop bath:** it should stop developing. **It keeps developing, and in the print she comes down the stairs to the lens.** The safelight flickers.
  9. **The hall, a last flash: she is at the lens**: a translucent veil, dark eyes, the window showing through her. Black.
- **Scare placement:** the last frame; small ones before it (the vapour, the empty stairs, the landing).
- **Sound language:** no score:
  - the hall clock and the view camera's shutter and dark slide;
  - the magnesium flash;
  - old stairs creaking under the cloth;
  - the darkroom's rocking tray, drips and safelight hum;
  - silence before the last flash.

## Facts and their limits

Checked from **web-search summaries only**: Country Life, the Museum of Hoaxes, The Black Vault, Mental Floss, The Line-Up, and encyclopedia mirrors.

| Claim in the video | Status |
|---|---|
| The most famous ghost photograph ever taken | Widely described as such; an opinion, said as the narrator's |
| Raynham Hall, Norfolk; September 1936; two photographers from Country Life magazine | Consistent: 19 September 1936; Captain Hubert C. Provand and his assistant Indre Shira (not named in the video) |
| They had taken one picture of the staircase and were setting up a second | Consistent with their account |
| The assistant saw a vapoury shape coming down the stairs, gradually becoming a woman | Consistent: Shira's account, "a vapoury form gradually assuming the appearance of a woman" |
| He shouted "take it"; the flash went off; the other man saw nothing | Consistent with the account: Shira called out, Provand took the exposure and said he saw nothing. The exact words vary between retellings, so the video paraphrases |
| Country Life printed it that December | Consistent: December 1936 (sources differ on 16 or 26 December) |
| Experts said the negative had not been touched | Consistent with the reports of the negative's examination at the time |
| Skeptics say double exposure, or something on the lens | Consistent: Joe Nickell and others argue double exposure; grease or a smear on the lens has also been suggested |
| A lady in brown had been reported on these stairs for a hundred years | Consistent with the reported sightings from the 1830s on (including Captain Marryat's); legend |

**Not used:**
- the identity claims, such as Lady Dorothy, as fact;
- the "glowing eyes" sighting details;
- the photographers' names on screen;
- any claim about the house today.

The print **keeps developing** only in our dramatisation. The figure at the lens and the landing silhouette are ours, not claimed.

## Care

- No living person is shown or named.
- The photographers (both long dead) are seen only from behind and are not named in the video.
- The skeptics' explanations are in the narration.
- Raynham Hall is a private home: no visiting framing, no dares.
- The figure is an original design from the photograph's description: a dress and a pale veil.

## Hidden rewatch details

1. **The figure is already in the print** in the first frame of the hook, before the second photograph is taken in the story.
2. **On the ground glass she is upside down**, as a view camera sees, so she "comes down" by rising up the glass.
3. **One window of the house is lit** when the car arrives.
4. **The photographer never looks up** until after the flash; he saw nothing.
5. **At the lens, the landing window shows through her veil.**

**Open question (pinned comment):** what was on the stairs?
