# Cancer: scientific scope and visual decisions

This is a 76-second original educational short about growth safeguards, immune evasion and the logic of some immunotherapies. It follows a **solid-tumor example**; it does not imply all cancers form the same kind of mass or follow one identical progression. It contains no personal diagnosis or treatment recommendation.

| Spoken claim | Visual treatment | Scientific distinction preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer starts in our own cells | An abnormal nucleus sits within a self-like outer cell | The colored disguise is a visual metaphor, not a literal color-changing mechanism |
| Cells respond to growth/repair/stop signals and can self-destruct | Signal contact, repaired DNA rung, neighbor contact and a damaged control cell's fragmentation | These are distinct safeguards, simplified and time-compressed; one depicted change does not mean one mutation always causes cancer |
| Genetic changes can disrupt safeguards | A DNA rung changes and a control connection breaks | Cancer generally involves accumulated changes; the cartoon does not specify an actual gene or sequence |
| Uncontrolled copies can displace healthy tissue | Visible two/four daughter stages, then additional clones and displaced neighbors | Division timing is explanatory time compression; this is not a literal mitotic-phase reconstruction |
| Immune cells can recognize/destroy abnormal cells | A control target is contacted, receives granules and fragments | This is a stylized cytotoxic interaction, not a phagocyte swallowing a germ |
| Some cancer cells evade detection or inhibit immune attack | Surface symbols change; a separate inhibitory contact stops an attack | Recognition and inhibition are different failure routes; the lock is a signaling metaphor |
| Some cells spread elsewhere: metastasis | Cell crosses tissue, enters blood flow and appears at another site | The movie shows one simplified route; not every cancer metastasizes, and the process has additional biological steps |
| Immunotherapy can help immune recognition/attack; some treatments release brakes | A Y-shaped antibody blocks an inhibitory contact and a target subsequently fragments | Checkpoint inhibition is one immunotherapy class, not all immunotherapy; no specific drug or universal response is claimed |
| Results differ | A responding target and a persistent target are contrasted | No cure rate, universal efficacy or individualized benefit is promised |

Established background references:

- National Cancer Institute: [Immunotherapy to Treat Cancer](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/immunotherapy)

## Artwork and animation architecture

The approved original medicine artwork supplies the clinician, microscope and prepared laboratory environment. Its source image is unchanged. New original cell artwork was generated as a sheet containing separately registered cytoplasm/membrane and nucleus components. Original PNG bytes remain unchanged; silhouette geometry supplies independent rendering regions. The source is not a reference-video frame.

Remotion animates membrane deformation, nucleus offsets, daughter-cell poses, surface markers, contacts, antibody paths, granules, fragmentation, red cells, flow, tissue fibers, field particles, microscope focus and prepared clinic layers. Camera/mask handoffs follow a cell, nucleus, contact, tissue wall or microscope context. Escape→disguise preserves the same staged pair without an extra wipe. No on-screen title, branding or production labels are included.

The enlarged old clinician deformation exposed matte seams. The final clinic view retains the original body pixels and uses local blinking, knob rotation, prepared background layers and slight reframing. The first forceful division skin also failed visually; the corrected version uses gentle deformation, registered nucleus movement, daughter poses and a narrowing bridge.
