[excited] Another possibility is that our expansion followed an earlier phase.

Some speculative models replace a singular beginning with a bounce. A contracting universe reaches extreme conditions, and new physics allows expansion to follow.

In that picture, our hot cosmic history has a previous chapter.

But drawing a beautiful bounce is much easier than showing that one actually happened. A convincing model must make physical sense and survive comparison with observations.

[short pause] We have not established that our universe bounced.

Then there is inflation: a proposed period of extremely rapid expansion very early in cosmic history.

Inflation can help explain several large-scale features we observe. But what drove it, how it began, and whether it happened in a particular form remain open questions.

It does not automatically explain why anything exists.

Some inflationary scenarios suggest that regions beyond our observable universe could develop different cosmic histories—sometimes described as other universes.

[suspenseful] So did every universe come from the same singularity?

[thoughtful] We do not know that other universes exist. We cannot establish a shared birthplace for them.

The multiverse is a set of speculative possibilities, not an observed family tree of reality.
