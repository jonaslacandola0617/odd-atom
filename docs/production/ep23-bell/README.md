# Episode 23: "Backwards" (v1)

**Delivery:** `assets/videos/ep23-bell-v1/ep23-backwards.mp4` (1080×1920, 30 fps, 57.67 s, H.264 two-pass tuned for grain, AAC 192k) with its poster `ep23-backwards-poster.png` (frame 60: the mourners with lanterns at the grave). Size and hash are in "Review of the encoded file". It has **no user approval** and **no listening pass**.

**The case:** the Bell Witch, Adams, Tennessee, 1817–1821 (#23 in `docs/stories/season-3-world.md`). A real, reported case: a farm family's account, published by M. V. Ingram in 1894.

## Why this episode looks and sounds like this

- **The user's note after Episode 22:** the season's structures were different, but the pictures barely moved; "only the narrator is telling the story … the video itself should be scary, the video itself should tell the story." This is the first episode built to that rule.
- **Form:** told **backwards**, scene by scene, each joined by a **film rewind** in which the previous scene runs backwards at seven times speed (a picture that literally rewinds): the funeral (December 1820) → the sickroom → supper (1819) → the children's room (1818) → outside (1817) → the first night.
- **Motion:** **every shot moves**: a track behind the mourners, a push into their turned faces, a creep from the doorway to the windowsill, an orbit round the supper table, a push through the children's doorway, a track along the outside wall, a turn into the corn, a slide round the bedpost; all handheld.
- **One visible event per scene (the mute test):**
  - **the funeral:** the coffin goes down on ropes; a laugh; a gust snuffs the lanterns one by one down the line; **every mourner turns to look past the camera**;
  - **the sickroom:** **a small vial rolls along the sill by itself** towards the bed; the hand on the quilt twitches; the candle leans away;
  - **supper:** every candle flame leans towards the **empty chair**, which scrapes back; on the wall, **the shadow of someone sitting in it**; the family turns to look;
  - **the children's room:** **a quilt slides off a bed, then is yanked to the floor** towards the door;
  - **outside, 1817:** **scratches appear on the logs ahead of the camera**; something unseen **pushes through the corn towards the house**;
  - **the first night:** **one scratch draws itself into a bedpost**.
- **Scare placement:** the front (the laugh and the gust at about 5.7 s), then several small ones; the ending loops into the funeral ("You know how it ends").
- **Sound language:** real-sounding effects from ElevenLabs (the laugh, the gust, the quilt, the scratching) with synthesized foley and a drone whose swells are **reversed** into every rewind.
- **Visual language:** a damaged old film print: sepia, grain, dust, vertical scratches, gate weave, projector flicker, date cards.

## Care

- John Bell and his family were real people: **faces stay in shadow or turned away**; no one is shown harmed; his death is not shown (a shape under a quilt, a hand).
- The children are shapes under sheets; nothing touches them but the quilt.
- The narration says plainly where the story comes from and that the original account is missing.
- The farm and graveyard are composites; no visiting framing.

## Hidden rewatch details

1. **The cabin's lit window** far behind the funeral is the same window seen from outside in 1817.
2. **The vial is already on the sill** before the camera finds it.
3. **The empty chair is at the head of the table**, and nobody sits down in it all scene.
4. **The second child's bed** is never touched.
5. **The scratches on the logs** have the same three-and-a-half stroke shape as the one on the bedpost.

## Still review passes

1. **The render page crashed** at the sickroom: a Remotion `interpolate` with a decreasing input range; fixed.
2. **The funeral camera was too close**: six black shapes filled the frame. It moved back 8 m and up, so the grave, the lanterns and the line of mourners read; the sky and snow were lifted so their turned heads read as silhouettes.
3. **The children's room, the outside and the bedpost were black**: `directionalLight`s aimed at the world origin, far from these sets, and `hemisphereLight` with only `args` lit nothing. They became ambient and point lights, and the point lights were raised to useful intensities.
4. **The corn was invisible**; it was lightened and the night sky lifted, so the stalks read against it.
5. **The bedpost scratch was a huge white comb**; it was made smaller, three strokes, and wood-coloured.
