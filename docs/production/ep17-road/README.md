## Folklore and its limits

Checked from web-search summaries only (Philippine Daily Tribune, "Where spirits dwell"; Inquirer on the balete; Wikipedia-derived pages on the tikbalang and kapre; the Sacred Texts copy of *Philippine Folklore Stories*).

| Claim in the video | Status |

| Taller than the bamboo, a horse's head on a man's body | Consistent: a tall creature with a horse's head and very long limbs; squatting, its knees rise above its head |
| It doesn't hurt you; it keeps you | A fair summary of the leading-astray belief (some tellings are darker) |
| Say "tabi tabi po" when passing big trees | Consistent: a common courtesy to unseen beings |
| Turning your shirt inside out releases you | Consistent: the remedy in the folklore |
| The rider, the store, the tricycle found 200 m from his house | Fiction |
| Rain under a clear moon | The "tikbalang's wedding" (rain while the sun shines), transposed to moonlight |
| The ember in the balete | The kapre, who shares these places (a hidden detail; not narrated) |

## Care

- The beliefs are treated as wisdom that saves the rider: he remembers, and the road lets him go.
- No comic tikbalang: it is tall, still and quiet, and it never hurts him.
- The store's name is invented; no real place, person or barangay is named.
