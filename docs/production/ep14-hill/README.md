## Facts and their limits

Checked on 2026-11-11 from web-search summaries only: Inquirer Business ("Haunted structures"), Inquirer News ("Baguio's 'haunted hill' @100"), The Aswang Project, Atlas Obscura and Wikipedia-derived pages. Full pages and primary records were not read.

| Claim in the video | Status |
|---|---|
| Built by the Dominicans on the hill more than a century ago | Consistent: most sources give 1913–1915, as a rest house (later a seminary or school) |
| In the war, people hid here | Consistent: civilians sheltered there during the Second World War. Accounts of the Japanese occupation differ (a garrison, or a place where prisoners were held) |
| Later a hotel, then abandoned to the fog | Consistent: a hotel from about 1973 to 1987, then abandoned. It is now a heritage park (Dominican Heritage Hill), declared a National Historical Site in 2005 |
| Caretakers hear knocking after midnight, plates and voices | Reported legend (caretakers' and visitors' accounts in features), not documented |
| Figures in the corridors, priests and nuns, without heads | Reported legend |
| "Historians have no record that it really happened" | Consistent: the beheadings of priests and nuns claimed in some versions are not documented in the sources found |

## Care

- The wartime suffering at the site is real. It is shown only as people sheltering by candlelight, with no harm, and under far, low booms.
- The beheadings are legend, and the narration says historians have no record of them.
- The figures have no wounds: an empty collar, an absence.
- The site is a heritage park. Nothing invites a night visit; the last line ("huwag ka nang maghapunan doon") is a local's warning, not a dare.
- No real person, priest or nun is named.
