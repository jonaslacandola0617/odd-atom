# Episode 21: "Watch the Chair" (v1), Season 3 opener

**Delivery:** `assets/videos/ep21-chair-v1/ep21-watch-the-chair.mp4` (1080×1920, 30 fps, 1,768 frames, 58.95 s, H.264 two-pass tuned for grain, AAC 192k) with its poster `ep21-watch-the-chair-poster.png` (frame 75: the chair by the window). Size and hash are in "Review of the encoded file". It has **no user approval** and **no listening pass**.

**The case:** the Enfield poltergeist, north London, 1977 (#21 in `docs/stories/season-3-world.md`). A real, documented, disputed case: police and investigator testimony, over 180 hours of tape, press photographs, and investigators who concluded some of it was faked.

## Why this episode looks and sounds like this (Season 3: no shared template)

- **Form:** ONE unbroken, locked-off take in real time, as if a camera had been left running on a tripod in the back bedroom. No cuts at all.
- **Scare mechanism: misdirection.** The hook tells you to watch the chair. **The chair never moves while you can see it.** It moves only during three tape dropouts (five frames of noise each), nearer the far bed each time, turning to face it. The narration ends "You were watching the chair. Weren't you?"; the landing light fails, comes back, and **someone is sitting in it**, facing the bed. A last dropout cuts to frame 0, with the chair back by the window.
- **Scare placement:** many small ones (knocks inside the wall, knocks that answer, a shadow on the landing, a rasp from the far bed, the clock stopping) and the last frames.
- **Sound language: diegetic only.** No score at all: everything could be on the tape.
- **Visual language:** a camcorder left running: REC light and counter, tape noise, three dropouts, a fixed frame, one room at real scale with practical light only (the streetlight through orange curtains, the landing light through the door, a passing car's headlights on the ceiling).

## Care

- The children are **never named and never shown**: blanket shapes, a little hair on a pillow.
- The family is not mocked; the skeptics' case is in the narration.
- **No real recording** of the case is used. The rasp is synthesized (a 72 Hz pulse train through vowel formants), with no words.
- The figure in the chair is a dark silhouette from behind: no face, no identity claimed.
- No address is given in the video; no visiting framing.

## Hidden rewatch details

1. The chair **only moves in the dropouts**: four positions, each turned a little more towards the far bed.
2. **The clock turns 1:00** exactly as "one in the morning" is said.
3. **A shadow crosses the landing light** while everyone in the room is in bed.
4. **The far bed stops breathing** during the rasp, then starts again.
5. **The picture over the far bed is left crooked** by the first knocks.
6. **The clock stops ticking** in the silence and never starts again.

**Open question (pinned comment):** who was sitting in the chair?

## Picture standard

- **Dark key:** every frame measured 8–15/255 mean luma (only the dropout frames are bright).
- **Restraint and realism:** one room at real scale (3.6 × 5.1 m, 2.4 m ceiling), 1970s wallpaper, lino, a sash window, candlewick bedspreads, a kitchen chair, a cassette recorder; motivated light only.
- **One figure, once:** a silhouette from behind for about 1.1 s at the very end.

## Sound (`scripts/ep21_voice.py`, `scripts/ep21_sound.py`)

- **Narrator:** ElevenLabs `eleven_v4`, **Jack – Deep Dark Horror Narrator** (`BVPhWQykET1TSvfGzrUr`), English, on the user's Starter account.
  - **Voices confirmed** with `creative_list_voices` before generating (both Jack and True are available).
  - **Takes:** one call on a new flow (`26b0ws5KUUylJK30Q4PP`) generated **both takes** at once (1,500 credits). Take 1 (48.35 s) is used; take 2 (50.68 s) is kept.
  - **Processing:** split into 8 lines; pauses inside a line capped at 0.45 s; **no tempo change**; a gentle compressor (3:1 above −24 dBFS, 5 ms / 150 ms) and a soft clip 12 dB over each line's RMS, because with no score the narration carries the loudness.
- **No score.** The bed: room tone, 50 Hz mains hum, tape hiss, the recorder's motor, the landing bulb's hum, a wall clock, breathing under the blankets, a far dog, a passing car, knocks (synthesized thumps through a wall filter), a floorboard, four dropout bursts with a chair leg scraping under the first three, the rasp, the bulb fizzing, a creak and a low settle under the weight in the chair.
- **Library (Curio, user-authorised):** `wood_stress` (the landing floorboard and the chair taking weight). Path and hash are in `public/assets/audio/ep21-chair-v1/PROVENANCE.json`. Stems are FLAC.
- **Mix:**
  - **−14.0 LUFS**; the decoded AAC peaks at **−2.2 dBFS** (tanh soft limiter, ceiling −2.35 dBFS).
  - **Presence** (phone band; the whole non-voice bed is measured in place of a score): **−21.6 dB during speech and −9.3 dB in the gaps.** This is quieter than the score target on purpose: a room's tone sits about 20 dB under a close narrator. The events themselves in the gaps measure about −3 to −8 dB against the voice (knocks, the first dropout), and the 3.2 s silence is near-digital silence with tape hiss.
  - **Line margins:** every line is about 20 dB over everything else (no lifts).
  - **Loop seam:** −16.2 dBFS in the first quarter-second, −15.7 dBFS in the last (the cut runs through a dropout on purpose).

**No listening pass was possible in this runtime**; presence and margins are measured.

## Still review passes

The room was lengthened to 5.1 m, the camera moved back and centred on the window, and the recorder moved to the bedside cabinet under the window, where its red light reads.
2. **The figure merged into black:** the chair's last position was at the far bed's foot, against a dark wall. It was moved so the sitter's head is silhouetted against the lit curtains.
3. **The landing light read as a grey slab** on the floor; its floor wash was narrowed and lowered to 8% opacity.
4. **The chair read too dark by the window**; its wood was lightened.
