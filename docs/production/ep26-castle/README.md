# Episode 26: "Post Three" (v1)

**Delivery:** `assets/videos/ep26-castle-v1/ep26-post-three.mp4` (1080×1920, 30 fps, 59.76 s, H.264 two-pass tuned for grain, AAC 192k) with its poster `ep26-post-three-poster.png` (frame 60: the corridor). Size and hash are in "Review of the encoded file". It has **no user approval** and **no listening pass**.

**The case:** the Castle of Good Hope, Cape Town (#26 in `docs/stories/season-3-world.md`). It is a real place with reported hauntings: footsteps, a woman in grey running through the corridors, a tall man leaning over the battlements, and the story of Governor van Noodt.

## Why this episode looks and sounds like this

- **Rules:** it is built to the motion rule and the no-gimmick-transitions rule (hard cuts only).
- **Form:** a night guard's radio log in the first person. The guard is a composite, not a real person, voiced by Jack.
  - His torch is the light, and his radio is always in his hand at the bottom right of the frame.
  - The radio's LCD shows his post, the time and the transmit/receive state. At the end it shows control's last message, in its own 5×7 pixel font.
  - Every shot moves: he walks, stops and turns. The castle from above drifts down.
- **The picture tells it (the mute test).** The scares are small and escalating, one per place:
  1. **The corridor (the hook):** a woman in grey runs out of a side doorway and away down the corridor, into the moonlight.
  2. **From above:** the castle's five bastions, the city and the mountain; **a window lights by itself**.
  3. **The courtyard:** he walks, then stops. **Two more footsteps** sound behind him. He turns, and **the door behind him is swinging shut** under its lamp.
  4. **The governor's room:** the portrait, the window, the desk. **A page of the ledger turns by itself**, then **the chair slides back from the desk, as if someone stood up**.
  5. **The ramparts:** in the wind, **a tall man leans over the battlements**. He straightens (the wind drops dead) and turns his head. The torch flickers, and he is gone.
  6. **The corridor again:** at the far end, **she is standing in the opening. She runs straight at him**; the torch dies; she passes in the dark. The torch comes back on an empty corridor.
  7. **The dungeon:** he goes down the steps; seven tallies are scratched in the wall.
     - "Dungeon's clear. I'm coming up." Then **the door slams behind him**, and he turns: shut.
     - The torch dies. In the radio's green glow, **control's message comes in: "RX CONTROL / POST 3. WHO IS / WITH YOU?"**
     - **Long grey fingers curl over the top of the radio** from behind it. Black.
- **Scare placement:** several small scares, escalating (the slate's plan for this episode); the biggest is the last.
- **Sound language:** diegetic, no score:
  - the radio's squelch, key clicks and message tone;
  - boots on stone;
  - the wind off the bay and the sea;
  - near silence where the picture frightens.
- **Visual language:** a guard's torch POV with his radio in his hand; the radio text on screen.

## Facts and their limits

Checked from **web-search summaries only**: Time Out Cape Town, Cape Town Tourism, Cape Town ETC, Cape Town Magazine, IOL, and blogs. **Most sources are tourism pages**, and some contain errors.

| Claim in the video | Status |
|---|---|
| The Castle of Good Hope, Cape Town; the oldest colonial building in South Africa | Consistent: built 1666–1679 by the Dutch East India Company |
| The guards before me said you'll hear footsteps | Reported: soldiers, staff and visitors report footsteps and voices, notably in the dungeon and the narrow corridors |
| 1729: Governor van Noodt sentenced seven soldiers to hang; one of them, the story goes, cursed him from the gallows | Sources differ on the year (1728 or 1729; April 1729 in one). The sentences are described as for desertion. The curse is legend, and the video says "the story goes" |
| The same day, the governor was found dead in his chair; history says it was his heart | Legend has him found dead at his desk (the same day or the next morning, by source); other accounts say a heart attack |
| Guards have reported a tall man leaning over the battlements | Reported in ghost-tour and tourism material (search summaries); not independently documented |
| A woman in grey, running through the corridors | Reported (the "Lady in Grey") |
| When they dug here, they found the remains of a woman; no one knows who she was | Reported: a woman's remains found during excavations; some sources say the sightings became rarer after. The link to the Lady in Grey is suspicion only, and the video does not claim it |

**Not used:**
- the bell-tower and black-dog stories;
- claims about the identity of the Lady in Grey;
- any claim that the curse was real.

## Care

- The hanged soldiers and the governor are **never shown**; no execution or death is depicted.
- The curse is told as a story and the heart as history.
- The guard is a composite.
- No member of staff or the military is named.
- The castle is a working museum and military site: no night-visit framing, no dares.
- The rooms are composites, not the castle's real layout.

## Sound (`scripts/ep26_voice.py`, `scripts/ep26_sound.py`)

- **Narrator:** ElevenLabs `eleven_v4`, **Jack**, English; one call on a new flow (`zpapeHc6ecmvVW0Ey4SU`) generated both takes (1,776 credits).
  - **Take 2** (54.0 s) is used: it is 1.1 s shorter, and the episode had to fit 60 s.
  - Even so, the first timeline was 68.9 s. To fit, the line "I walk it alone. Two till dawn." was cut, inner pauses were capped at 0.32 s, and the voice runs at atempo 1.05.
- **ElevenLabs sound effects** (`eleven_text_to_sound_v2`, 4 prompts × 4 variations, 272 credits): the radio's squelch, boots on stone, a woman running in a long skirt, and a dungeon door slamming. They were **picked by measurement, not listening** (`prompt.json`).
- **Library (Curio):** `heavy_hinge` and `steel_latch` (the courtyard door), `paper_light_turn` (the page), `wood_stress` (the chair), `cavern_lowwind` (the dungeon).
- **Synthesized:** corridor air, the wind off the bay and its drop, the sea, water drops, torch clicks, cloth, the chair's scrape, the radio key clicks and message tone, a breath, low hits.
- **No score.**
- **Mix:**
  - **Loudness:** −14.0 LUFS; decoded AAC peak −1.28 dBFS.
  - **Bed presence** (phone band; the whole non-voice bed): −12.2 dB during speech, −6.8 dB in the gaps.
  - **Line margins:** every line at least 7.3 dB over everything else (l06, in the rampart wind), no lifts.
  - **Loop seam:** −17.9 dBFS at the head. The last 0.4 s is black and digital silence, so the loop restarts from silence.
