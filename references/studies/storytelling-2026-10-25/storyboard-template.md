# Plan the explanation before animating the scene

Use this as an internal shot/sequence brief. These fields are production notes, not on-screen text. Keep the approved artwork and transition quality; plan the action that those assets must support.

| Decision | Fill in before detailed animation |
|---|---|
| Viewer question | What is the viewer trying to understand at this moment? |
| Intended understanding | One concrete relationship, expressed as a sentence with a verb. |
| Starting condition | What must be established so the change can be recognized? |
| Subject / actor / variable | What the viewer follows, and how its identity stays clear. |
| Trigger or intention | What starts the process, or what the actor is attempting. |
| Interaction | The force, contact, decision, binding, transfer or interruption that actually changes the state. |
| Consequence | What visibly becomes different and remains different long enough to read. |
| Comparison anchor | What stays stable so the difference can be judged. |
| Attention order | What the viewer should notice first, second and third; how gaze, entrance, contrast and staging support that order. |
| Emotional role | Why the event matters to an observer or actor; use a meaningful pose/reaction or hold where appropriate. |
| Next beat | The question or consequence that motivates the following composition. |
| Transition handoff | Which object/action continues into the next view, retaining the approved transition principles. |
| Necessary language | Terms, units, statistics, unseen mechanisms and qualifications that images alone cannot supply. |
| Evidence category | Observation, simplified mechanism, analogy or hypothesis; draw and label it accordingly. |
| Required artwork | Poses, facial states, contact hands, working parts, deformable surfaces and environment states needed to make the event happen. |
| Final voice timing | Use the actual Knox phrase/pauses to stage preparation, interaction, result and a readable settle. |

For a human action, block the look/attention, intention, body effort, hand/prop action and response together. Keep contact with ground or props convincing. For a scientific diagram, preserve identity, show a source and destination, establish the changed interaction and retain its consequence. Give background motion a stated role: atmosphere, scale, obstruction, transport, evidence of a result, or the event itself.

Review the rough sequence with narration muted and narration captions hidden. Ask what changed, what visibly caused it, and what fact still needs language. Someone unfamiliar with the brief can provide a useful independent reading; the analyst's own mute review is not a substitute for that feedback. Restore the narration and check that it clarifies rather than contradicts the image.

If removing a movement leaves the explanation unchanged, classify its contribution honestly: atmosphere, rhythm, attention or emotion may still justify it. If it does none of those jobs, remove it. If the causal action is missing, design that action instead of filling the shot with more drifting objects.

Before an expensive full render, make the proposed before/action/after states visible in the animatic and inspect the actual movement between them. A polished still and a smooth transition do not establish that the intervening scene explains its intended relationship.
